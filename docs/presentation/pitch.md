# Mission Forge — pitch

1. **Mission Forge.** We’re Try2Fly from Oral. We built a game about a question space engineers face every day: what can you actually give up to make a mission work?
2. **Fire Watch.** The forest service needs observations from orbit. A thermal sensor records useful data, but that also costs mass, power and money. The mission has six resources to balance, including data storage and the link back to Earth.
3. **Build.** In the hangar, players see each design choice on the spacecraft and check it against four mission segments. They can send a design with a warning and find out what that warning means in flight.
4. **One change.** Here are two model runs with the same instruments, events and decisions. The difference is the antenna. X-band delivered more than three times as much data. It also cost about twelve kilograms, forty-five watts and 1.37 million simulated dollars.
5. **Fly.** The team has four roles with different priorities. A crisis can interrupt the flight, and the team votes on its response.
6. **Debrief.** Players get an explanation of which decisions mattered. We also compare the scale of the exercise with Landsat 9 and link to NASA’s specifications. Landsat has different scientific requirements and life-cycle costs.
7. **Try it.** Scan the link to open the public game without installing anything. In Fire Watch, try to improve the amount of data that makes it back to Earth.
